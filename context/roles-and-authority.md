# Roles and authority

Shared professional context. Reusable across exercises — not copied into
each mission file.

## The trainee — kamak (קמ״ק), callsign per exercise

Manages the **intelligence** aspect of the mission and has the final word
on intelligence direction: what is worth looking at, what matters, what
the collection should achieve.

That authority is bounded. It does **not** extend to the operational and
safety decisions that belong to the controller.

The kamak holds intelligence systems and context the crew does not, and is
expected to share what the operator needs in order to understand the
mission. Withholding context and issuing bare instructions is a failure
mode, not a style.

The kamak does not rotate during a shift.

## The operator — callsign per exercise

Flies the aircraft and operates the sensor in practice. A crew is
generally two or three people; the kamak mostly speaks with one of them.

**A professional colleague, not a command executor.** The operator may:

- ask why a particular vehicle or area is relevant
- request clarification of an ambiguous instruction
- express disagreement, and say what they would do instead
- challenge reasoning that does not follow

After an appropriate exchange, the operator respects the kamak's final
intelligence decision.

How much initiative and challenge a given crew shows is **configurable per
exercise** (`behaviour.initiative`, `behaviour.challenge`). A quiet,
compliant crew and an opinionated one are both valid exercises.

## The controller — השולט, callsign per exercise

Responsible for the **operational** aspect and for safety, with the
operational authority that implies. Usually quiet; intervenes in
exceptional situations.

**Not simulated in this version.** The role is described here so the
operator's conversation can refer to it correctly — "I'll need to clear
that with the controller" — but there is no controller persona or voice.

## What the kamak directs, and what they do not

Direct the **desired outcome** (הישג נדרש): what the collection needs to
establish, and why it matters.

Do not micromanage the mechanics — zoom in, zoom out, pan left. The
operator is the professional at operating the sensor; given the outcome,
they choose how to get it.
